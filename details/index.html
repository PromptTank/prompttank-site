Partners ask

Six questions, every time.

What if someone pastes a Social Security number anyway?

The room stops, tells them exactly what has to come out, and will not proceed until it does. That behavior is installed, not hoped for, and your staff can read the rule that produces it.

What if it makes something up?

It will, and the rooms are built on that assumption rather than against it. In the tax room every authority it produces carries a verify-before-use flag no matter how confident the wording sounds, because the model cannot tell its wrong answers from its right ones. In bookkeeping it runs the percentage and refuses to tell you whether something is material, because that threshold was never written down at the firm. The rooms are designed to be checkable, not to be trusted.

We already pay for ChatGPT. Why is this not redundant?

That is why it is inexpensive. We are not selling you software. We are putting your firm’s rules inside seats you already own, which right now run on whatever each person happens to type.

My people won’t use it.

Usually true, and it is why we build from interviews instead of templates. There is nothing to learn and nothing to open — your staff describe what they need the way they would to a colleague, and the room runs the matching prompt itself. If someone is unsure, they type “hi” and pick from a menu. Each department also gets a one-page guide. Total staff time across the whole build is about twenty minutes each.

Why interview inside the AI instead of sending a questionnaire?

A written answer to “walk me through it” is three sentences that skip the middle, and the middle is where the decision they make without noticing lives. The self-interview asks one question at a time and follows up on the step that got compressed.

What does a peer-reviewed paper have to do with my firm?

It is why the rules layer is built the way it is: a rule that cannot be pointed at, versioned and reviewed is not a rule. Nothing in the paper is being sold to you.
